Inside a black hole, there is no matter, no fields, no radiation—and also no space, no time, and no coordinate systems.

The collapse of a red giant star into a more compact object inevitably generates angular momentum. This results in rotation at immense velocities. This rotational energy is conserved within the resulting black hole, generating centrifugal forces that initially sustain the event horizon but may subsequently cause a rupture in spacetime from within the black hole itself. An ontological void—a region of non-existence devoid of any coordinate system—is formed inside. This framework resolves several major problems for this specific case: the nature of the singularity, quantum gravity, the information paradox, and the question "What holds up the horizon?". A breakdown of this insulator implies the infilling of the void with spacetime.
